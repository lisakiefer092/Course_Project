# The Environmental Impact of our Food
### The global food system is responsible for about 30% of greenhouse gas emissions, occupies 40% of the habitable land, and uses at least 70% of the freshwater available. However, different food items have different environmental impacts, and this dashboards aims to show this in detail for various exmaples. Users can find out which food items requrie the most resources and can therefore be mindful in their food consumption to minimize negative consequences.
## Data
#### The Data was pulled from the "Our World in Data" website. It is in three different CSV files, one considering the greenhouse gas emissions of food items, the other the water usage, and the third the land area used. The "environmental score" is achieved through a normalization of the data, giving within each data set each food item a score from 0-100, with 100 being given to the food item with the highest value.
## Dash Board 
#### The dahsboard consists of three tabs in which the data is visualized. In the second tab there is a horizontal bar graph showing the different environmental categories, with each category color-coded to be more visually appealing. The third tab displays a vertical graph showing the score within each environmental impact category (Greenhouse gas emissions, Land use, and Water use - each category is displayed in a associated color). The average of these three scores is the overall environmental impact score, displayed in as a star rating and a category of "Excellent, Good, Moderate, Poor, Very Poor". 
## Audience
#### The immediate audience consists of college students. Considering the circumstances, the tone of the presentation is ligth, with the presentation not trying to be overly accurate but rather easily understandable and visually appealing such that the broad message comes across. The intent is that the students at the end of the presentation have a good sense of what the environmental impact of different food items look like and where this impact is coming from. Nonetheless it is to be made sure that the presenter knows what they are talking about, especially about what exactly is displayed, what the units are, and how the environmental score was achieved.
